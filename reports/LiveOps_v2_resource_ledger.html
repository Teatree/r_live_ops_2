What changed since the last edition

The previous edition of this report ran on the 26 Jul – 3 Aug exports, which turned out to carry three data problems. This edition is a full rewrite on the re-exported, cleaned files and a new window: 2 – 10 Aug. Nothing from the old edition should be quoted; specifically:

The +183 HC net swing at 100+ is gone. It was driven by the one-sided hc_excluded_players rule (12% of Variant dropped, 0% of Control) plus 1–2 whale accounts. With symmetric cleaning (0 exclusions in both arms in this window), the clean read is the opposite: 100+ HC net is down, 36.8 → 30.3 per player-day.

Rainbow Maker numbers were bug-inflated. A reward-duplication / over-config bug has been fixed upstream and the correction applied to the exports: in-window it removes 2,101,408 HC and 561,424 pass tokens, essentially all from the Variant (7.5% of its uncorrected HC; Control impact: 103 HC). Every figure in this report uses the corrected series — verified: the ledger's RM totals match the corrected, not the uncorrected, telemetry columns.

Night Sky's framing changed. The old window suggested a 50% → 100% rollout doubling. In this window Night Sky simply does not run for Control (§03, H2) — the exposure ratio is ~5,900:1, so all NS deltas measure adding the event, not re-pricing it.

02

The overall picture

The Variant cut the gross hard-currency faucet 12–20% in every playing segment and rebuilt part of it out of events — but the spend side contracted almost exactly in step, so net HC per player-day is nearly conserved everywhere. The redesign shrinks how fast coins flow through the economy, not how solvent players are: balances sit lower in the Variant but are flat across the nine days, and zero-balance rates stay under ~1% in every segment.

Three levers do almost all the work. The saga nerf removes 68–72% of saga HC and returns it as Unlimited-Lives minutes; the calendar swap kills four one-off events and replaces them with a denser set (Night Sky, chuck event, Rainbow Maker, Archery Arena, kite) that over-replaces them at every depth — but cannot offset saga at the top; and Rainbow Maker floods the Dream-Pass token lane while the per-level pass reward was cut (§07). The one segment that gains is A. 0: it had no saga income to lose, and Night Sky pays for attendance rather than level completion.

03

Q1 — Highlights, with mechanisms

Twelve things worth flagging. Every headline number below was either re-derived by an independent verification agent from the raw CSVs, or matches this report's own primary derivation; framing corrections from verification are already folded in.

H1A. 0 is the only segment whose HC income rose — +12.3%+12.3%

What: HC gain per player-day 43.0 → 48.3 in A. 0 while B–F fall 12–20%. Its spend rose +18% with it (29.2 → 34.5), all through extra-moves retries at an unchanged ~9% buyer rate, and net is identical (13.9 both arms).

Why: the zero-completion bucket earns almost no saga HC (0.15/pd in Control), so it dodges the nerf entirely — and Night Sky is streak-gated, not completion-gated: it adds +12.9/pd here (the segment's #1 source at 26.7% of its HC), more than covering the Daily-Gift nerf and the killed events. Free event HC passes straight through to retries: the paid share of A. 0's spend falls 24.2% → 15.4%. Engagement follows the money — +6.8% minutes, +9.8% completes, +2.3pp win rate.

H2Night Sky ran only for the Variant in this window — the delta is exposure, not economyexposure ~5,900:1

What: Variant logged NS HC on all 9 days in all 6 segments — 4,008,500 HC, 16.9/pd, reaching 30.1% of player-days (67% at 100+, where it pays 77.2/pd). Control logged 240 HC on 12 gainer-days — 0.005% of its player-days, at most 2 gainers on any day.

Why: the Variant calendar schedules Night Sky daily (its output is stable at ~445k HC/day, CV 5%); the Control calendar simply doesn't contain it. The trace Control rows look like a handful of leaked/misassigned clients, not stragglers. Control's pre-window trend shows no NS-scale source switching off at 2 Aug, so this likely holds before the window too.

So what: every NS number in this test measures adding the event (its HC alone is ~16.5% of the Variant's free HC; UL minutes and boosters ride on top), not its reward tuning — there is no Control baseline against which a ladder change could be read. The arm configs would settle it in a minute (§09).

H3The saga nerf is progressive: deep players lose the most per claim−71% HC

What: saga HC per player-day falls −68…−72% in B–F (F: 266.7 → 76.9). Per gainer-day the cut steepens with depth: −42.6% at B down to −70.9% at F — while participation barely moves at the top (F: 99.0% → 98.1% of player-days) and collapses at the bottom (B: 40.5% → 22.6%).

Why: the v2 ladder removed HC from many chapter rewards and shrank the rest. Deep players still hit an HC-paying chapter almost daily — at ~29% of the old amount; shallow players now often hit only non-HC chapters, so their loss appears as vanished gainer-days rather than smaller claims.

H4Saga swapped coins for Unlimited Lives — and the life economy shrank around itUL +41% at 100+

What: saga UL minutes rise +71…+77% in every playing segment (F: 188.8 → 327.0/pd) against the HC removal; total Unlimited-Lives minutes reach 606/pd at 100+ (+41%). Knock-on: lives spent collapse −47% at F (28.0 → 14.9/pd) and lives purchased fall 34–39% — failures during UL time consume no life.

Why: a literal reward-mix swap in the saga ladder. Note the accounting asymmetry: UL Lives has no spend sink at all (12 trivial use-rows against 53.2M minutes granted), so the swap moves value from a fully-metered resource into an unmetered one. Whether those minutes are used or expire unused is invisible in these exports (§09).

H5Net HC is conserved — the redesign cuts velocity, not solvencynet ≈ flat

What: HC spend fell almost exactly in step with gains in every bucket (see §02), leaving net per player-day within a few coins of Control everywhere (F: 36.8 → 30.3 is the largest gap). Median EOD balances sit 2–16% lower in the Variant but are flat from day 1 — the gap does not widen — and zero-balance share stays at 0.5–1.0% in all twelve segment-arms.

Why: the marginal HC sink is the +5-moves retry, and players spend what they receive — spend absorbed 70–100% of the gains change in every bucket. One nuance: "conserved" is an absolute-coins statement; as a rate, B and D's small net accrual roughly halved (5.4 → 3.1, 9.3 → 4.3). The cut lands on hoarded surplus (F p90 balance compresses ~1,400–2,050 → ~1,100–1,380) rather than on liquidity — no depletion spiral within the window.

H6The calendar swap over-replaces the killed events — everywhere except against saga+152.9 new−70.1 killed

What: at 100+, the four killed events (river rush, red, bomb, collection) + race fall −70.1 HC/pd while the new set (night sky +77.2, chuck +25.3, rainbow +24.2, archery +15.7, kite +10.6) adds +152.9 — the swap alone is HC-positive at every depth. The segment declines are saga (−189.8), the Daily-Gift nerf (−6.3), offers (−17.3) and small residuals.

Why: the v2 calendar is denser and its events pay better — but event HC scales with participation while saga HC scaled with play depth, so the replacement is structurally flatter across segments (see H12).

H7Dream Pass tokens explode ×2.7 — 90% of the delta is Rainbow Maker, and it's broad-based×4.55 at 100+

What: DPT per player-day rises ×2.1 (A. 0) to ×4.55 (F: 40.0 → 182.1); Rainbow Maker supplies 90–100% of the delta in every segment and is the #1 token source in all six (49–70% of supply). Distribution got less concentrated: 100+ top-10 share fell 7.5% → 3.8%, median tokens per player rose 50 → 236.

Why: RM's milestone ladder pays tokens at nearly every step. But this is the ledger's event lane only — the core per-level lane (invisible to the export) was cut at the same time, and the total pass faucet actually fell (§07). These are corrected numbers; the duplication bug would have added another ~561k tokens.

H8The pass paradox: tokens up ×2.7, milestones down −7.8%faucet −6%

What: average milestone reached fell 7.33 → 6.76 all-players (participants 13.4 → 12.5); track completions (milestone 30) fell −18% relative. Yet the ledger shows tokens tripling. Resolution in §07: the ledger only sees event tokens — the per-level core lane, 94.8% of Control's pass income, was cut ~24% season-to-date, and it outweighs Rainbow Maker's +153.6 per pass player.

H9A stealth Daily-Gift nerf: −35% per claim, in every segment−35%/claim

What: daily_reward HC per gainer-day falls ~−35% uniformly across all six segments while claim rates are untouched (max |Δ| 0.5pp), and the gift's non-HC contents (UL minutes, boosters) are identical per claim in both arms.

Why: a flat config cut to the gift's coin line only — small per segment (−4 to −6.4/pd) but one of the few levers applied uniformly rather than progressively. Worth calling out because it is invisible inside the aggregate 'core' decline.

H10IAP revenue is down −14.3% — payers held, baskets shrankfragile p≈0.05

What: 9-day revenue 21,744 → 18,642 on near-identical payer counts (2,455 / 2,476; conversion 2.11% both arms) — pure ARPPU (−15%). Concentrated at E (−33.9%) and F (−34.3%), partly offset by C (+23.4%). Paid HC acquired per active at F fell −36%.

Why: consistent with the sink story — fewer extra-move wall-hits means fewer coin-pack top-ups; the Variant's free grants substitute purchased HC (visible directly in A. 0's paid-share drop). Treat as unproven: daily gap −330 ± 434, t≈2.3, and Aug 9–10 are Control-side whale days; 9 days and ~140 payers/arm at the top is thin (§09).

H11Win rates fell where the wallet tightened — and rose where it loosened−1.3…−2.4pp C–F+2.3pp A. 0

What: level win rate drops in every bucket C–F (F: 77.1% → 74.7%) with completes per active down 1.4–3.8% at flat-to-higher time spent; out-of-moves rates rise. A. 0 moves the other way: +2.3pp win rate, +9.8% completes.

Why: mechanical coupling to H5's rationing — fewer HC-funded continues means more attempts conceded at the wall. Engagement time doesn't fall (failed attempts still consume minutes), so this reads as friction redistribution, not churn pressure — retention is flat in C–F (§08).

H12Booster supply rotated from saga to events — a regressive redistributioncasuals up · 100+ down

What: the Variant cut saga's booster payouts 55–100% per booster (except bomb, untouched) and backfilled with event faucets absent from Control (RM, night sky, archery, chuck). Because saga paid proportionally to play depth and event ladders pay roughly flat per participant, B-tier players gain (red +46%) while 100+ loses (red −37%, chuck −37%). Spend followed supply 1:1; per-booster detail in §06.

Q2 — Where each resource comes from

Across 13 material resources × 6 segments, the dominant gain source changed in roughly half of all cells — and Rainbow Maker alone captures 20 of those flips. The matrix below shows the Variant's #1 source and its share per cell; an orange edge marks cells whose owner changed vs Control.

Where words beat the matrix: HC — in Control, saga owns HC in C–F (26–38% of segment income); in the Variant, player_level_up "takes over" everywhere without growing at all (−1.4% absolute) — ownership passed to it by subtraction. In A. 0 the new owner is Night Sky (26.7%). Dream Pass tokens — Rainbow Maker is #1 in all six segments, rising to 70.5% at 100+: the one monoculture the redesign created, and a single point of failure for pass pacing (this is also the lane the RM bug lived in). Slingshot — RM becomes #1 in all six segments (23–34%). Stable cells — bomb, UL bomb, lives and SeasonPassToken kept their owners in every segment in both arms; lives stay coin_purchase-dominated in B–F while A. 0 uniquely leans on ask_for_lives (~46%). SeasonPassToken is a >92% season-pass-event monoculture but economically trivial (~0.12/pd) — the real pass currency is DreamPassToken.

The structural read: Control's economy had two concentrated always-on faucets (saga at up to 67% of a booster's supply, daily gift); the Variant splits most resources across 4–5 comparable sources — top-source share for HC, red and chuck now stays under 31% in every segment. More levers for LiveOps; noisier per-source telemetry.

Niche but sharp — the 2× pass multiplier: SeasonPassMultiplier2x gains rose ×6.0 (77,850 → 467,465 minutes), with Rainbow Maker paying 90.3% of the Variant's supply as a free milestone reward. Control's supply came from river_rush (43%) and two paid offers (~28% each). The paid offers' absolute volume held roughly flat in-window (~21.6k each → ~24.3k / ~19.6k), so this isn't observed cannibalization yet — but RM now gives away ~10× what those two SKUs sell, which makes them largely redundant and compounds the RM-token flow into the pass (§07) with a multiplier from the same single event.

05

Q3 — The 100+ net position

The premise doesn't survive the clean data: there is no 100+ net-gains inflation. HC net per player-day is 36.8 (Control) → 30.3 (Variant), −17.6%; booster net 3.52 → 3.19, −9.2%. The old +183 swing was the exclusion bug plus whales — with symmetric cleaning, gains (−137.2/pd) and spend (−130.8/pd) fell nearly in lockstep. What remains to explain is the composition: which sources carry the Variant's gains, and what actually drove both sides down.

The spend side: one sink, fewer buys per buyer

Extra moves are 96–97% of all 100+ HC spend in both arms, so the spend decline is the extra-moves story: −119.8 of the −130.8/pd. The decomposition is clean — price is exactly 100 HC per purchase in every context in both arms; buyer breadth barely moved (88.8% → 87.6% of player-days buy at least once); what fell is purchases per buyer-day: 6.69 → 5.47 (−18%). Same players, same price, fewer retries. The discretionary sinks were cut far harder than the compulsory one: pre-level booster shop −78% (RM and Night Sky now grant those boosters), powerup shop −38%, lives −39% (substituted by UL minutes, H4).

Concentration check (distinct players, 1,750 C / 1,740 V): HC gain top-1 share is 1.65% / 0.57% (C/V) and top-10 5.2% / 4.02%; spend concentration is similar. Nothing whale-driven survives in either arm — the median player IS the story (per-player net p50: −33.7 C, −28.8 V; both arms' median player is a net HC spender). The lone concentrated cell is the tiny star-booster sink (top-1 32.5% of ~4.4/pd) — a small-base artifact.

Boosters at 100+: gains 34.8 → 29.4 and spend 31.2 → 26.2 fell together; saga alone removed −11.6 of booster supply, RM restored +5.8. Net moved only −0.32/pd. No booster inflation either — the per-booster picture is §06.

07

The Dream Pass paradox

The ledger says tokens tripled (§03 H7); the pass says progression fell. Both are true, because the ledger is blind to the biggest lane: level-completion token grants never reach the resource export — and that lane is 94.8% of Control's pass income (241.8 of 255.1 tokens/pd by client telemetry). The Variant cut it to 204.0, so the total pass faucet fell 6.0% while its visible (event) slice tripled.

At matched play (137.8 vs 135.1 saga completes per pass player), the decomposition closes exactly: pass score −296.5 = core lane −449.1 + Rainbow Maker +153.6 − other events 1.0. Per completed level the core reward fell 12.83 → 9.77 points (ratio 0.761), and that ratio is stable at 0.75–0.78 in all five play-matched buckets — the signature of a config change, not of population shift. Rainbow Maker's tokens do credit the pass at face value (uplift across RM bands meets or exceeds the 1:1 prediction), they just don't cover the core cut.

A mid-experiment step, flagged: the daily core-per-level ratio ran at ~0.51 through 29 Jul — matching the halved SP_v2 panel the simulation models (R = 0.495) — then stepped to ~0.85 from 31 Jul (blend 0.783, which reproduces every season-to-date ratio in these files). Either the config was softened mid-test or a season rollover carried a milder panel. Whichever it is: the live per-level pass reward no longer matches the sim's SP_v2 assumption, and season-to-date pass numbers mix two regimes (§09).

Where it lands: average milestone 7.33 → 6.76 (−7.8%); +2.3pp more players stuck at milestones 1–5; the milestone-30 cap share falls 8.0% → 6.6% (−18% relative) — the cut compounds along the track, so it bites hardest exactly where paid Pass buyers live. Participation barely moved (54.6% → 54.1%).

08

Test health

The arms are comparable populations: 115,920 vs 116,438 assigned (+0.45%, within randomization noise), activation near-identical per bucket, conversion 2.11% in both arms. Bucket migration is not a confound at this horizon — active-player bucket shares move at most 0.21pp, so nine days is too short for the Variant's slower completion rates to demote players in volume.

Three things to keep in view: (1) the assignment export carries quota-like artifacts (B. 1-9 assigned exactly 39,581 in both arms with D1 identical to four decimals; E at round 10,000/9,900) — population comparisons hold, but don't lean on per-bucket assigned counts; (2) the revenue gap (§03 H10) is real in this window but statistically fragile; (3) the one significant retention movement is A. 0: D7 12.4% → 11.3% (z = −3.2) — the segment the Variant made richer plays more per active day but comes back slightly less. Worth watching, since A. 0 is also the segment the redesign helps most on paper.

09

Q5 — Ambiguities & what I'm less sure about

  1. Night Sky exposure is inferred, not configured. The ledger shows ~0% Control exposure and the pre-window level test agrees, but ledger absence can't distinguish "not scheduled" from "scheduled, zero uptake" (implausible given 30% Variant uptake) — and ~2–12 Control accounts did play it somehow. The arm calendar configs would settle it in a minute, and a lot of §03 H2's framing rests on it.
  2. The mid-test core-SPT step (§07). Per-level pass rewards ran at ~0.51× Control until 29 Jul and ~0.85× from 31 Jul. Softened config or season rollover? Every season-to-date pass number in this report blends the two regimes, and the simulation's SP_v2 panel (R=0.495) now matches only the early regime.
  3. Revenue (−14.3%) is directionally consistent but fragile: t≈2.3 on nine daily observations, whale-sensitive (Aug 9–10 are Control-side whale days), ~140 payers per arm at the top. A longer window or a per-payer bootstrap would firm it up; I would not ship a pricing decision on it.
  4. UL Lives and DPT are unmetered on the consumption side. UL Lives has no sink at all and DPT's pass-claim consumption never reaches the ledger — so "net" for those resources is bookkeeping, and the requested expiry/overlap split for UL minutes was not derivable from these exports. The §07 telemetry files cover DPT's fate; UL minutes' fate (used vs expired) remains unknown.
  5. Assignment-export artifacts: B. 1-9's identical assigned counts and D1 in both arms, E's round 10,000/9,900, and D's +3.1% imbalance (z=2.7) look like quota enrolment or a partially copied export column. Population-level conclusions are unaffected; per-bucket assigned counts are not to be trusted as raw randomization output.
  6. Jigsaw window-clipping distorts the UL boosters both ways: Control's run sat fully in-window, the Variant's extends past 10 Aug — roughly half of UL chuck/bomb's decline is schedule, not config. Bomb Ballet has the opposite bias. Event-level UL comparisons need schedule-aligned windows.
  7. Balances differ from day 1. The Variant's ~16% lower median HC balance at 100+ predates the window — a pre-window ramp (players have been in-arm since before 2 Aug) that these files can't decompose. The flat-gap + sub-1% zero-share reading (no depletion) is solid; the level difference's origin isn't.

The remaining ~50 supporting findings carry one derivation path each (analyst + this report's own extraction), not an adversarial second pass.
