01

The overall picture

The Variant cut the core faucet and rebuilt it out of events. Hard currency is down 6–8% in every segment; almost everything else is up.

The mechanism is consistent across buckets: saga_progression loses roughly 60% of its HC and river_rush disappears entirely, while Rainbow Maker arrives from nothing, Night Sky doubles, and Bomb's Ballet and Red's Challenge add more on top. The replacement is not like-for-like — the new faucets pay in tokens, unlimited lives and boosters far more than in coins, so Dream Pass tokens more than triple at the top while HC still ends the window down.

Spend fell too, in every segment and by roughly the same proportion as gains — except at 100+, where it collapsed. That single divergence is what produces the net-position swing, and section 03 takes it apart.

02

Highlights

Eleven things worth flagging, each with the mechanism I think produced it. The first two change how the whole test should be read.

H1

Night Sky doubled in every segment — with an unchanged reward table

×1.95 avg

What: dreamheist_event HC per active player-day — 1-9 2.4→4.8, 10-19 8.8→17.1, 20-39 19.7→37.7, 40-99 40.7→79.9, 100+ 71.3→125.1. It is now the largest single event faucet in the game.

Why: this is exposure, not economy. HC per gainer is flat or slightly down (100+ 265.7→233.9, ×0.88; every other bucket ×0.96–1.00), while the share of player-days that earn from it lands on almost exactly double: 26.8%→53.5% at 100+, 28.3%→58.0%, 31.0%→61.0%, 28.8%→56.4%, 15.3%→30.1%. The clean reading is that Night Sky ran at roughly 50% rollout in Control and 100% in the Variant. The ladder didn't change; the audience did.

So what: any model that treats Night Sky as neutral between the arms is wrong by a factor of two, and half of the "new events replaced the nerf" story is actually "the existing event was un-diluted".

H2

The saga nerf swapped hard currency for unlimited lives

HC ×0.39UL Lives ×1.25

What: saga_progression at 100+ pays 193.4→74.5 HC per player-day, but 260.3→326.1 minutes of Unlimited Lives. Same at 20-39 (HC 39.8→17.5, UL Lives 56.8→73.3). Gainer share is untouched (100+ 0.99→0.98) — the same players clear the same nodes.

Why: the reward table was re-mixed, not just cut. Coins were pulled out and replaced with a time-boxed consumable that expires if unused — cheaper for the economy, and it never shows up as a sink. One line was deleted outright: saga's powerup grant went 1.87→0.01 at 100+, gainer share 0.88→0.00.

Also note the scaling: per gainer the cut is progressive — ×0.68 at 1-9, ×0.59, ×0.51, ×0.44, ×0.39 at 100+. Deep players lost proportionally more, which is why the HC drop is flat across buckets even though saga is a much bigger share of their income.

H3

Dream Pass tokens more than tripled — Rainbow Maker is a token firehose

+262% at 100+

What: 70.0→253.3 tokens per player-day at 100+; +235% at 40-99, +195% at 20-39, +168% at 10-19, +122% at 1-9. Rainbow Maker alone supplies 186.6 of the 253.3 — 74% of all token income at 100+, and 56–68% in every other bucket.

Why: RM's milestone ladder pays tokens at nearly every step and the event runs for days; nothing else on the calendar pays them at that density. Watch-out: tokens drive Season Pass tier progression, so this is a direct tier-inflation vector — the paid and free tracks will be reached far earlier than the old calendar assumed.

H4

One Rainbow Maker day paid 926 HC per gainer to a third of the cohort

×10 vs its other days

What: on 30 Jul, 93 of 280 100+ players (33%) took 86,100 HC from RM — 925.8 per gainer, against 50–95 on its other days. The next day, 22 players (7.6%) took 731 each.

Why: the deep tail of the milestone ladder clearing at instance end — the grand-prize band. It is working as designed, but it means RM's average is a poor summary of RM: a third of the cohort collects most of its value, and the tail is where the HC actually sits. If the ladder's top milestones are re-priced, that day moves more than any other lever in the event.

H5

One account per day accounts for most of the 100+ spend collapse

−176.6 HC/day

What: the pre-level booster shop (map_pre_level_booster) went from 176.8 HC per active player-day to 0.19 — that one sink is 74% of the entire 239-point HC spend drop. In Control it ran on 33 spender-days across the nine days, at 11,840–20,523 HC and 102–118 purchase events per spender-day. The Variant side is three spender-days with one purchase each.

How thin, exactly: the daily export reports spenders per booster tier, and on eight of nine days it is one spender per tier (0.27–0.36% of ~350 players) with near-identical event counts across the three tiers — 105/100/100 on 26 Jul, 278/271/272 on 27 Jul. That is one account buying one of each in a loop. Two days show two. So: 1–2 distinct accounts a day, at most 13 across the window, and on 27 Jul a single account made 821 purchases worth 123,450 HC.

Why it is not a feature change: the shop still operates in the Variant — other buckets keep buying from it (40-99 has more spender-days in the Variant, 36→43) and no renamed sink appears in the Variant's 100+ ledger to absorb it. What changed is who is in the 100+ cell, not whether the shop exists. At ~110 purchases a day this also does not look like ordinary play; worth deciding whether it belongs in the analysis at all.

H6

The 100+ cell isn't comparable across arms — for two independent reasons

12% of Variant dropped−16% player-days

An HC cleaning rule fires on one arm only. In the summary workbook's per-day counts, hc_excluded_players removes zero players from Control in every bucket, and 0.2–0.8% of the Variant in every bucket except 100+, where it removes 12.0% (281 of 2,343 player-days). The CSV player-day counts line up with the cleaned population, so these exports inherit it. A filter that trims extreme HC accounts from one arm and not the other will remove precisely the kind of account described in H5 — from the Variant, while Control keeps its own.

And the cohort itself moved. 100+ player-days fell 3,183→2,679 while 20-39 gained 6.5%. Buckets are defined by 7-day level completions and the Variant lowered the pass rate, so players slip down a bucket. Per-bucket comparisons are partly comparing different people — strongest exactly where the sample is smallest.

So what: together these make the 100+ HC comparison the weakest number in the read-out. The rest of this report rests on cohorts of 20k–60k player-days with symmetric treatment; the 100+ net swing does not.

H7

Target Day tripled its Unlimited Lives payout, identically in every segment

×2.75 – ×3.07

What: ArcheryArena UL Lives per player-day — 1-9 2.10→6.46, 10-19 2.29→6.31, 20-39 2.16→6.35, 40-99 2.00→5.90, 100+ 1.94→5.33.

Why: a uniform multiplier across five very different cohorts is the signature of a config edit, not behaviour — the UL Lives line on the Target Day reward table was raised roughly threefold. Worth confirming it was intended: it is the largest single relative increase of any unchanged-schedule event.

H8

The calendar swap is visible as clean on/off switches

4 in4 out

What (100+ HC per player-day): in — Rainbow Maker 0→63.0, Bomb's Ballet 0.02→10.1. Out — River Rush 39.4→0.0, Photoshoot 11.3→0.1, bomb_event 14.5→0.9, Jigsaw 4.2→0.0. Photoshoot also took 30.2 UL Lives per player-day with it.

Why: nothing subtle — these events left or joined the calendar. Recorded here because River Rush's removal alone is a 39-point hole at 100+, roughly a third of the saga cut, and it is easy to attribute that to the nerf by mistake.

H9

Red's Challenge doubled at the top — by frequency, not richness

×2.10 at 100+

What: 16.2→33.9 HC per player-day at 100+, 9.1→17.1 at 40-99.

Why: HC per gainer is flat (172.2→168.4) while gainer share doubles (9.4%→20.2%). More instances on the calendar, same ladder — the same signature as Night Sky in H1, at smaller scale.

H10

The zero-completion cohort lost the most HC, proportionally

−16.1%

What: A. 0 players complete no levels yet still take 22.3 HC and 24.4 minutes of UL Lives per day. Their HC fell 26.6→22.3, the steepest drop of any bucket, driven almost entirely by daily_reward (12.75→9.24).

Why: their income is pure login/social/ads — no event ladder reaches them — so a change to the daily reward table hits them undiluted, and none of the new events compensate. They are 30,647 player-days, the second-largest cohort in the test, and they spend 2.1 HC a day against 22.3 earned: a near-pure faucet with no sink. Worth a decision either way.

H11

Boosters split in opposite directions by depth

+11% at 1-9−28% at 100+

What: booster gains per player-day move +11.2% / +7.8% / +0.4% / −13.3% / −27.9% from shallow to deep.

Why: two effects pointing opposite ways. Shallow players pick up boosters from the early milestones of the new events (RM, Bomb's Ballet, Night Sky), which they can actually reach. Deep players lose the saga-node boosters (H2) and the pre-level boosters they used to buy with HC (H5) at the same time — the same event that removed their spend also removed the goods it bought.

03

Where each resource comes from

One structure explains almost all of it: shallow players are fed by scheduled and social faucets, deep players by event ladders and saga nodes. Every resource shows the same gradient, which is why this is a paragraph and a table rather than thirty charts.

Read the table down a column and you see one cohort's diet; read it across a row and you see the gradient. Hard currency is the clearest case: at 1-9 the biggest single source is the daily reward (24%), and no event breaks 10%; from 10-19 upward Night Sky is the largest source in every bucket (16% → 23%), with saga and level-ups the only other double-digit lines. Unlimited Lives runs the same way in reverse — Flock Rush leads at the shallow end (19%) and saga takes over monotonically with depth, ending at 57% of all UL Lives at 100+.

Three resources are one-source stories. Dream Pass tokens are Rainbow Maker everywhere, and its share rises with depth (56% → 74%) because deeper players reach more of its ladder. Powerups are Rainbow Maker in every single bucket (19–24%) — a new event became the primary powerup faucet in one release. Lives are the exception to the whole gradient: asking friends plus buying with coins is 78–89% of life income in every bucket, with only the ad-granted share sliding down with depth (20% → 10%) as players stop needing free lives.

The two booster families split the way H11 predicts. Pre-level boosters come from Rainbow Maker, offers and Night Sky at the shallow end, and from saga nodes at depth (35% at 100+). Unlimited pre-level boosters come from the daily reward at 1-9 (40%) and from Bomb's Ballet at depth (25–26%).

And where the spending went

The honest headline: the 100+ net improvement is not the Variant making players richer. Their gross income fell 8.5%. Three quarters of the spend drop is one account per day that exists in Control and not in the Variant (H5), sitting inside a cell where a one-sided cleaning rule has already removed 12% of the Variant and 0% of Control (H6). Treat the +182.8 swing as unproven.

The part that is real: extra moves, −59.7 HC per player-day, and it is broad. Sink-rows per player-day are essentially flat (112.3% → 110.1% — a player can buy at several move tiers, so this counts rows, not people), the price per purchase is unchanged at 100 HC, and what falls is purchases per buyer, 5.49 → 5.06 (−7.8%). Same people, same price, fewer buys. That is consistent with smaller balances (P50 −9%, P75 −12%, P90 −10%) and rationing — a second-order effect of the gains cut, and the part of the swing that would survive a rerun.

Boosters

On this data the 100+ booster net position does not inflate — it falls, 19.5→14.8 per player-day, because booster gains (76.2→55.0) dropped further than booster spend (56.7→40.1). Both sides shrink for the reasons in H11 and H5.

What does inflate is Unlimited Lives: 459.9→568.0 minutes per player-day, +23.5%, and zero against it in the spend ledger — there is no UL Lives sink at all. Of the Variant's 568, 57% comes from saga nodes, 8% from level-ups, 7% from Rainbow Maker, 4.4% from Night Sky and 2.9% from Bomb's Ballet. So the booster-side inflation is really the saga reward-mix swap (H2) plus the new events, landing in a resource that is granted in time and never consumed on the books.

What would change my mind

  • The one-sided exclusion (H6) is the first thing to fix. Zero Control players are dropped in any bucket; 12% of the Variant's 100+ is. Until the same rule runs on both arms, no 100+ HC number here — gains, spend or net — can be attributed to the Variant. This is a query fix, not a re-run.
  • The 100+ sample. 2,679 player-days, ~300 players a day, and the biggest single number in the analysis rests on 33 spender-days from 1–2 accounts. A distinct-player count, or the same read with those accounts excluded from both arms, would settle H5 immediately.
  • Bucket migration. Completion buckets are an outcome the Variant changed, so the cohorts are not fixed. A read against a pre-assignment bucket would remove the confound.
  • The Night Sky rollout assumption (H1). Doubled participation with flat per-gainer payout is consistent with a 50%→100% rollout, but a scheduling change would look the same in this data. Someone with the arm configs can confirm in a minute, and a lot depends on it.
  • Nine days vs seven. These files cover 26 Jul – 3 Aug; the summary workbook covers 27 Jul – 2 Aug. Booster aggregates in particular are defined differently there and will not tie out.
