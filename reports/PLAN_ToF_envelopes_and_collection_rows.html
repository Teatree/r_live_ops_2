Two Models, One Album

Tower of Fortune hands out envelopes nobody opens, and two different methods disagree about what finishing a set is worth. Both are the same kind of fault.

Commit 1

Tower of Fortune actually plays its runs

The economy summary already credits Tower of Fortune with card envelopes. The card-opening simulator has never heard of it. So those envelopes are counted in one place and never opened in the other — and a player who should be filling their album slightly faster isn't.

The reason is shape, not oversight. Every other event has a flat prize list: finish third, get this. Tower of Fortune doesn't — you climb a ladder of doors, one of which hides a Pig, and you either cash out or lose the lot. The card sim only knows how to read flat prize lists, so it reads nothing here.

What I'll build

The card sim will play Tower of Fortune for real, one run at a time, using the tickets it already tracks. It looks like this:

tickets
The log already banks whole tickets, day by day, from every source that pays them. Those are the tickets the runs spend — nothing new is invented, and nothing is asked of the Tower of Fortune model. That also means the runs on the sheet can never contradict the ticket column sitting right next to them.
each day
If the player opened the game and holds at least one ticket, they play — up to Runs per Active Day, which is 2 today.
one run
Roll the survive chance at each door. Buy a continue when they can afford one and are willing. Stop at their cash-out stage. If they make it, they bank the whole ladder; if a Pig stops them first, they bank nothing.
envelopes
Only the envelopes go into the collection totals — the coins, boosters and pass tokens on that ladder already live in the economy sheet's own Tower of Fortune row, and counting them twice would be worse than not counting them at all.
day 29
After the album closes there's nowhere to put a card, so envelopes stop — same rule every other source follows. Tickets keep flowing, because the event itself is always on.
Worked example — 10-19 non-payer

Their cash-out stage is 9. Getting there means passing two coin-flip doors and a three-in-four, so their chance of banking anything is 1.5% per run. They can afford about 8 runs across the season.

chance a season shows no ToF envelope at all = (1 - 0.015) ^ 8 = 88% chance it shows some = 12%, and then TWO at once (a 1-star and a 2-star)

So roughly nine seasons in ten will show no Tower of Fortune envelope, and the tenth shows a pair of them on one day. That is not a rounding artefact — that is the event. Averaged over many players it comes to the 0.126 + 0.126 the economy sheet already reports, which is how we'll know the two agree.

And a Tower of Fortune row appears in the per-source tables on Col_Cards_Totals even where it reads 0.00. A zero there is the finding: it says the envelopes you put on that ladder are worth almost nothing to a mid segment, because they almost never survive to collect them.

How I'll know it's right

  • Over many seeds, the card sim's Tower of Fortune envelopes equal the economy sheet's Tower of Fortune row. Every segment, both payer types.
  • A player with no tickets plays no runs — and a player whose tickets all arrive on day 33 plays none either.
  • Push a segment's survive chance to 100% and every run banks; drop the cash-out stage to 1 and the pot is the first stage only. Then put both back and the original numbers reproduce exactly.
  • No envelope from Tower of Fortune lands after day 29, while tickets still do.
Commit 2

One method for what a finished set is worth

Two pieces of code answer “what do set and album completions pay?”, and they disagree by a factor of two to a hundred. The economy sheet uses a shortcut formula. The card sim plays it out properly. The card sim is right.

The shortcut assumes every card you draw is an independent lottery ticket. Four things in the real game break that assumption, and all four make you finish sets sooner:

  • Your Album Set Skew weights, which deliberately push draws toward the sets you haven't finished. The shortcut ignores them completely.
  • Both pity systems — the one that forces a rare card, and the one that forces a card you don't own yet.
  • Star chests, which buy extra envelopes with duplicate stars.
  • Cards come out of a pack without going back in. The shortcut puts them back.

The code even says so about itself: “these rows are therefore a floor, not a midpoint.” The album row is worse than a floor. It multiplies about seventy probabilities together, which collapses to almost nothing, so it reports zero album rewards for every segment — while the card sim finishes albums routinely for your two biggest spenders.

Why 0.2 and 25 are so far apart — 10-19 non-payer

This segment draws about 32 cards a season. Set 1 needs 9 specific ones out of 72.

shortcut every card is an equal lottery ticket P(own all 9) = tiny -> 0.2 coins real sim your skew sends 3x the draws at Set 1 while it is unfinished, pity forces an unowned card after 3 dry packs, and stars buy a chest or two -> Set 1 completes in half of all seasons -> 25 coins

The gap isn't a bug in either piece of code. It's that the shortcut was never told about the levers you've been tuning.

What I'll build

Delete the shortcut. Have the economy sheet run 20 real card seasons for each of the ten segment-and-payer pairs and average what they actually collected. The number stops being an approximation of the card sim and simply becomes it — so the two can never drift apart again, which is the whole point.

fixed dice
The same seed on every recalculation, so a cell never changes value when nothing changed, and two calendar versions are compared on identical luck. Edit a reward and the number still moves — only the randomness is pinned.
every album
Whatever a season actually finished counts, first album or third. This falls out for free and it's why the album row stops reading zero.
cost
200 simulated seasons per full refresh. I'll measure it and tell you the real number before this ships.
Two things this moves that you should expect

Your headline gain goes up. These are two of the twenty-nine rows in EcoGainsSim, and they feed the META column on Sim per Segment and the net projection on the daily sheet. For a 40-99 payer, roughly +200 coins from sets and +160 from albums per earner. I'll write the full before-and-after into the commit so the jump is on the record.

Commit 1 makes commit 2 slightly bigger. Tower of Fortune envelopes mean more cards drawn, which means a few more sets finished. That's a real effect, not double-counting — but it's why ToF goes first, so the collection rows are measured once, against the finished card sim.

How I'll know it's right

  • The economy sheet's two collection rows equal the card sim's set and album rewards, across all ten segment-and-payer pairs, within sampling error. This is exactly the check that would have caught the gap in the first place.
  • Change a value in Set Rewards and both sides move together, by the same amount. Then put it back and the original numbers reproduce.
  • Turn the album skew off and set completions fall on both sides — proving the economy sheet is now genuinely reading your levers rather than a formula that ignores them.
  • Two recalculations with nothing changed give identical numbers.

What I am not doing

  • Not touching the Tower of Fortune cash-out model. That stays a hard target stage, as you decided on the 3rd — commit 1 only teaches the card sim to read it.
  • Not putting Tower of Fortune coins, boosters or pass tokens into the collection totals.
  • Not changing the pig odds, the skew, or the chest settings. If the numbers above look wrong to you, those are the knobs — but that's a design call, not this plan.
