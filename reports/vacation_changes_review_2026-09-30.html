Vacation Changes Review

Compared: engine/vacation_changes/*.gs (6 files) against the engine/ HEAD of 14 Sep, and VACATION_CHANGES_COLLECTIONS_UNDER_NEW_CALENDAR.xlsx against COLLECTIONS_UNDER_NEW_CALENDAR (latest_1).xlsx, cell by cell. Every number below was produced offline by running the relevant engine on a dump of the relevant workbook (harness/_probe_album_matrix.js, paired seeds); the evidence files are in analysis/out/vacation_review/. The colleagues' own summary was used as a checklist, not as the source of truth.

The colleagues rebuilt how envelopes hand out cards (rarity picked first, per-envelope weights, no repeated card in one envelope), re-configured the pool and the album, re-priced envelopes, and started a Tower of Fortune rework. The card-sim code change is sound in its core idea and is what keeps album completion in the intended range; around it sit one dead code path, one missing guard, a calendar that was edited but not re-precomputed, and an envelope valuation that does not describe what the simulation actually pays.

The verdict in six lines

  1. Album completion is unchanged overall and shifted toward payers. Pre-vacation 4.43 % of the population finished the album; the intended new state gives 4.52 % (±0.13). Payers 6.4 % → 7.1 %, non-payers 3.4 % → 3.1 %. 100+ PAYER 34 % → 51 %, 40-99 PAYER 38 % → 43 %.
  2. Their 5.15 % headline is real but ran on a stale calendar. cal_new was edited (Kite 5 → 4 instances, Photoshoot removed, Bomb's Ballet doubled, Hatchling shifted) but cal_parsed was never re-precomputed, so every number they produced used the old schedule. On the intended calendar the same state reads 4.5 %.
  3. The new draw logic is the load-bearing change. The new pool and album config under the old draw would have pushed completion to 19–21 %; the rarity-weighted two-stage draw brings it back to 5 %. The two must ship together.
  4. One pity path is dead code. For envelopes with PityForceHighestRarity = FALSE (the 4-star pack, table [0, 0.33, 0.66, 1.0]) the pity never fires: targets are keyed by rank number and looked up by rarity name. Runtime-confirmed.
  5. A sheet without the new RarityWeights column makes every card the lowest rarity. Album completion then reads 0 % in 8 of 10 cells and looks like a balancing result. One guard line. (An earlier draft of this review claimed heavy players exhaust the 298-copy pool and run on a silent infinite pool; that was wrong — the pool is rebuilt when the album completes, see C4.)
  6. The envelope prices in item_vals (25 / 45 / 110 / 220 / 335) assume odds the sim does not produce. Recomputed on their own unit scale with the odds the engine actually uses, the 3★–5★ envelopes are worth ~60 % of the authored figures. Those prices are what the coin cuts were anchored against.

2. Code changes

All in CardOpenings.gs unless stated. Line numbers refer to the vacation copy.

C1 · lines 217, 343, 1279–1400

Two-stage, rarity-weighted card draw

Good ideaone consequence not carried through
What
drawOne now calls drawOneDualRandom: the rarity is picked first with weight = (copies of that rarity left in the pool) × (the envelope's RarityWeights entry), then a card inside that rarity by copies × chapter weight. A RarityWeights column was added to PACK DEFINITIONS.
Their reason
"1 star envelope should not have same odds of giving 5 star golden as 5 star envelope like it was." True of the old sim, where rarity was purely a pool property and every envelope drew from the same distribution.
Assessment
The right shape, and it is the change that makes the rest of the config work (§6). Two things to keep in view. First, the effective odds are weights × copies, not the weights: the 5-star envelope's [0, .025, .25, .40, .35, .15] gives a 6★ card 5 % of the time and a 5★ 22 %, because 6★ has 14 copies against 44 for 4★. The colleagues describe this correctly in their note, but their envelope valuation ignores it (K3). Second, the odds drift as the pool depletes — by design, and now steeper because rare copies are few.
C2 · lines 1497–1560, 1700

No repeated card inside one envelope; pool decremented after the draw

Good
What
Every draw filter now excludes cards already in this envelope (!drawn.includes(k)); the pool copy is removed as the last step; a Set check logs a console.error if a duplicate ever slips through.
Assessment
Correct — the old sim could deal the same card twice in a 6-card envelope when it had many copies. Small, real fix.
C3 · lines 1428–1460, 1622–1660, 1783

Pity rewritten — and half of it is dead

Needs a fixsemantics need agreement
What
The within-envelope consecutive-miss counter (the 3 Aug ruling: "no mod at first, then 80 %, 80 %, guaranteed", reset on a hit, fresh every envelope) is gone. In its place: a counter per envelope tier that survives across envelopes, escalating each time an envelope of that tier yields zero new cards and resetting when one yields any new card; the roll happens only on the last slot, only if the envelope has no new card so far (or, for forceHighest, no new card of the top weighted rarity), and it chases a new card of the target rarity. The old dry-streak pity (PITY_CONFIG) is commented out. Their note: "Pity system was operating incorrectly in simulation, corrected simulation, no tweaks to values were made."
The bug
pityTargets() returns, for forceHighest = FALSE, an object keyed by rank number (out[rk] = true); the draw filter looks it up by rarity name (targets[rarityOf[k]]). The lookup can never match, so the pity for the 4-star envelope — the only tier with a non-trivial table and FALSE — does nothing. Runtime check on the vacation engine and workbook, 300 seasons × 2 cells: forcing the 4-star table to [1,1,1,1] leaves the share of 4-star envelopes with zero new cards at 24.4 % → 24.1 %. The same forcing on the name-keyed TRUE path drops the 5-star share from 9.6 % to 0.2 %.
Also
The escalation condition (an envelope with no new card at all) does not match the attempt condition for forceHighest envelopes (no new card of the top rarity), so the 5-star pity practically never leaves probability 0 early in a season — a 6-card envelope almost always contains some new card. Whether the client really works this way is the question for Ville and Janne; the harness gate that encodes the 3 Aug semantics is permanently red under this code.
C4 · lines 1345–1400, 1650–1660

Pool exhaustion fallbacks: a higher rarity, then an unlimited pool

Low impact — corrected finding
What
When an envelope's weighted rarities have no copies left, the fallback scans upward and hands out whatever rarity still has stock (a 1-star envelope can pay a 5★ or 6★). When nothing is left at all, unlimitedPool = true treats every card as having one copy.
Correction
The first version of this review read "players who drew more than 298 cards" as players running on the unlimited pool. That was wrong: the pool is rebuilt the moment the album completes (line 1757), so drawing every copy means owning every card, which is completion, which resets the pool. Checked directly: of the 400 simulated 40-99 PAYERS, 125 drew more than 298 cards and all 125 had completed album 1 — they were in album 2. Same at 100+ (150 of 400, all completers). The unlimited-pool branch is effectively unreachable.
What remains
The upward fallback is real but narrow: it fires only when every rarity an envelope is weighted for is exhausted, i.e. a player missing only rare cards opens a small envelope near the end of the album, and it then pays them a rare. The old engine did the same in substance (it drew whatever copies remained). Worth a line in the sheet's notes so nobody is surprised by a 6★ out of a 1-star envelope; nothing to decide.

K3 · item_vals row 3 · PackConfig rows 86–145

Envelope prices 16/24/32/40/48/56 → 25/45/110/220/335 coins ("5★ ~8×")

Needs rework
How they were built
A per-rarity "card unit value" (1★ 0.154 … 6★ 2.541, a 16.5× spread; the whole album sums to 43.95 units) is multiplied by the envelope's weights and its card count, then scaled at ~50 coins per unit and rounded. The old table was a flat 8 coins per card.
Three problems
(1) The weights used are not normalised (they sum to 0.93–1.30), so envelope value scales with the sum of a row rather than a distribution. (2) The 5-star row in the valuation block (0 .025 .25 .35 .30 .20) is not the row in PACK DEFINITIONS (0 .025 .25 .40 .35 .15). (3) The copies multiplier the engine applies (C1) is missing, so the valuation assumes a 5-star envelope pays a 6★ card 20 % of the time; the sim pays it 5 %.
Recomputed
Same unit values, same 50 coins per unit, but the odds the engine actually uses: 1★ 21, 2★ 38, 3★ 68, 4★ 121, 5★ 207 coins against the authored 25 / 45 / 110 / 220 / 335. The three upper envelopes are priced ~1.6× above what the simulation delivers on the colleagues' own scale.
Why it matters
These prices are the "envelope value anchoring" the coin cuts on Rainbow Maker, Kite and Night Sky were made against (K9–K11); over-priced envelopes justify over-cutting coins. Separately, the 16.5× rarity spread is a perceived-value premium — the album's actual set and album rewards are worth roughly 650–900 coins, not the ~2,200 the unit scale implies. That premium is a legitimate design number; it does not belong in the coin-equivalent table the economy sums over (every value-gain total, the ToF gain/spend curves and Sim per Segment's value columns now carry it).
K4 · item_vals U2:U3 · ToF_model, ToF_St_Dist

ToF_Value = −15.8 coins per ticket

Wrong direction
What
=AVERAGE(ToF_model!M12:M16): the mean "net cumulative" of stages 1–5 in a spreadsheet model of the pig ladder that comes out negative at every stage (−5.6 at stage 2 to −1,439 at stage 19). A ticket is therefore valued at minus 15.8 coins.
Assessment
A negative value for a granted item means "receiving this costs the player coins": every ladder rung that pays a ticket now reads worse than paying nothing in the helper valuations (BB_v2 column Z, event_totals). The continue spend it encodes is already a sink in the economy model, so it is counted twice. The side model also disagrees with the engine's own run walker (tofRunOnce_), which banks positive expected value at the authored cash-out stage. Averaging stages 1–5 is arbitrary. Recommend: ticket value = the engine's expected banked value per run, or zero until agreed.

The three coin moves and what they add up to

  • Rainbow Maker −230 coins per ladder (K10), landing as −458 per 20-39 PAYER and −758 per 100+ PAYER, because the cut milestones (m16, m20) are the ones mid players actually reach.
